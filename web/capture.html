Filming a room that reconstructs well

Before anything can be trained, the app has to work out where the camera was for every frame. That step fails when frames don't overlap or there's nothing to track, and no setting can fix it afterwards. Footage is the biggest factor in quality.

Length

  • Aim for 2–3 minutes per room. Presets pick a fixed number of frames (Preview 100, High 200, Max 250), so a longer video spreads them further apart and they stop overlapping.

Movement

  • Move slowly and smoothly. Don't swing quickly from one area to another.
  • Walk the edge of the room facing in, then do a second loop through the middle facing out towards the walls.
  • Add passes at a low and a high height.
  • Overlap a lot: every part of the room should stay in view for a few seconds. End near where you started.

Camera

  • Lock exposure, focus and white balance if your camera allows it.
  • Use the main (1×) lens, not the ultra-wide.
  • 30 fps or higher keeps motion blur down.

Light and surfaces

  • Turn on all the lights and keep them constant. Avoid patches of sunlight that move.
  • Stand back from plain walls and doors. A close-up of a blank surface gives the software nothing to track.
  • Windows, mirrors, screens and glossy floors confuse it too. Cover or avoid them where you can.

Coverage

  • Film the corners, the ceiling edges and behind furniture you want to keep. Anything never seen will be missing or blurry.
  • Include something of known size, such as a door or a tape measure on the floor, for setting real-world scale later.

People and privacy

  • Keep the room empty while filming. Moving people leave ghosts.
  • If you film somewhere sensitive, get permission and keep personal information, notices and screens out of shot.

Everything stays on this computer: the footage is processed locally and never uploaded.
